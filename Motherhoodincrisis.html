motherhood in crisis

Motherhood in Crisis Dylan Sutton In the first chapter of Motherhood in Crisis, the creators use illustration as well as photography and text to depict a very intimate, dangerous, and devastating experience of a mother giving birth in an impoverished location with little resources. The combination of different formats like photography, illustrations, and video allows the reader to understand not only what happened but also actually inserts them into the moment with the mother. I think the most effective use of the illustrations is putting the reader into an intimate moment of the story that could not be captured on camera. By doing this, it preserves the mother's privacy by not showing real images of her in these vulnerable moments but still drives home the powerful moments that she experienced by giving us a stylized depiction of it. The story gives us illustrations of the actual birth as well as dramatic moments of the mother going through her grief, which would not have been ethical or possible to have captured with real photographs, which allows the reader to go along with her on this emotional journey and feel like they were in the room as it occurred. The photographs at the end are able to show us the real environments we have just seen and put real faces to the people we have just grown to empathize with throughout the story. The images and videos feel more informative and are used to wrap up the story, while the illustrations bring more emotional depth to the story. By using dramatic color and heightened and stylized visuals, the drawings are able to show emotions like fear, uncertainty, urgency, and sorrow that might be harder to capture in just a single photograph. By using illustrations, the creators are also able to compress long periods of time into just a few panels. For example, a two-day-long scene of the mother in labor is shown in just 5 panels. The use of colors to create lighting within the illustrations is very important to the effectiveness of the emotions being conveyed within each panel. For example, in the beginning of the comic, the woman has just begun giving birth; in these scenes, the orange and brown colors are used to create a warm and bright lighting of sunlight through the windows. In the scenes after the child has died as well as when her husband is comforting her, there is a significant tonal switch in the panels. They use cold blues and whites to create a dramatic moonlighting and darkness that conveys the sadness and grief the mother is experiencing. These comics show that not only is the choice to use illustrations an artistic choice, but it can also be a very powerful tool for storytelling and depicting emotional realities and sensitive topics such as maternal health that might not be able to be told as effectively in its absence. If the story were told through only photographs and texts, it would have been emotionally empty, and the reader would feel much more disconnected from the people and message.

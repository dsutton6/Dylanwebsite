The Rising Risk

W. J. Hennigan’s New York Times article “Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It” presents its readers with the real possibility of a threat that many people in our country have dismissed for a long time. By using a mix of factual reporting, history, and vivid illustrations, he argues that the possibility of nuclear conflict not only could be imminent but preparation for such a disaster has grown increasingly urgent in the past few years. In the article he debates the assumption that nuclear conflict between countries is inconceivable and presents information that proves that political tensions are currently very high. For example hennigan provides shocking facts such as “In the fall of 2022, a U.S. intelligence assessment put the odds at 50-50 that Russia would launch a nuclear strike to halt Ukrainian forces if they breached its defense of Crimea” as well as Nearly 200 hospitals in Ukraine have been identified as go-to facilities in the event of a nuclear attack. Thousands of doctors, nurses and other workers have been trained on how to respond and treat radiation exposure. And millions of potassium iodide tablets, which protect the thyroid from picking up radioactive material linked with cancer, are stockpiled around the country.” His reporting of this information leaves almost no room for debate on whether or not this threat is a possibility in the eyes of our world leaders. After presenting this information, the article then prompts us to “imagine a Nuclear weapon is dropped.” in bold letters surrounded by a sea of a dramatic red background. As we progress through the article we are shown shocking interactive images of a bomb being dropped and an intense explosion erupting causing devastating destruction to our world. In tandem with these images we are given vivid descriptions of the horrors of nuclear warfare being shown to us through these graphic illustrations such as “Screams for help — and for death — can be heard everywhere, but help is not on the way. Finding a doctor or a nurse is nearly impossible. Most medical workers in the immediate area are dead or injured. Those who survive are quickly overwhelmed”.

The use of these illustrations along with text to create strong descriptive storytelling really creates an almost cinematic presentation of a powerful message that all people need to be aware of in our current political climate. This article would not have been as impactful if it had been just text of facts and descriptions. The text provokes a fear in the reader but along with the illustrations we are given a visual of what one might actually experience in a nuclear fallout. These images heighten that sense of fear and add a shock value to this information that might not be possible with just text. By the end of the article we are shown more interactive illustrations of dead flowers, and bombs being sent across the planet, paired with text giving us the realities of a world post nuclear war. This article is incredibly effective by leaving the reader speechless after the images they have just seen and the information they have been subjected to. It serves as a wake up call to the world and the current geopolitical state of our world.
